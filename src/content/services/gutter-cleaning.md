Gutter cleaning is the removal of leaves, grit, and roof debris from the troughs and downspouts that carry rainwater off a roof, so water reaches the ground instead of overflowing at the fascia. **In Ventura County the standard advice to clean gutters once in the fall is wrong.** Coast live oak, the dominant native canopy tree across the Conejo Valley and the Ojai Valley, is evergreen and sheds its old leaves in late winter and spring, not autumn. That drop lands in the middle of the rain season. Aloha Window Bros cleans gutters across Ventura County and Santa Barbara County, and we schedule most homes twice: once before the first storms, and once again after the oaks finish dropping. Quotes are free and on-site.

## Key Takeaways

- **87% of the annual rain here falls between November and March**, and February alone is the wettest month at 3.26 inches (NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals, Oxnard Ventura County Airport).
- **Coast live oak sheds its old leaves from February to April**, per the US Forest Service, which is the same window as the heaviest rain.
- That overlap is why one autumn cleaning is not enough on an oak-shaded property here.
- Dry gutter litter is also an ember trap. The National Fire Protection Association lists clearing roofs and gutters as a first-zone action.

## Why Ventura County Gutters Clog at the Wrong Time of Year

Most gutter advice on the internet is written for the eastern United States, where deciduous trees drop in October and November and the rain is spread evenly through the year. Neither half of that is true here.

**The rain is compressed into a short season.** According to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration's 1991 to 2020 climate normals for the Oxnard Ventura County Airport station, the area averages 12.93 inches of precipitation a year, and it arrives in a narrow band:

| Month | Normal precipitation | Share of the year | Oak leaf drop |
|---|---|---|---|
| October | 0.46 in | 3.6% | no |
| November | 0.71 in | 5.5% | no |
| December | 2.08 in | 16.1% | no |
| **January** | **2.92 in** | **22.6%** | no |
| **February** | **3.26 in** | **25.2%** | **yes** |
| **March** | **2.30 in** | **17.8%** | **yes** |
| April | 0.69 in | 5.3% | **yes** |
| May to September | 0.51 in combined | 3.9% | no |

June through September together average **0.17 inches**, about 1.3% of the year. Effectively, the gutters do nothing for a third of the year and then handle everything in about ninety days.

**The trees drop at the wrong end of that season.** Coast live oak (*Quercus agrifolia*) is an evergreen, which leads a lot of homeowners to assume it does not shed. It does. The US Forest Service Fire Effects Information System describes its phenology plainly: "Leaves emerge from February to April; and old leaves are shed at the same time." Roughly 90% of the tree's stem elongation happens in March. So the year's heaviest leaf load arrives in February and March, which are the first and third wettest months on the table above.

The practical consequence is that a homeowner who cleans gutters in November has clean gutters for the December and January storms and clogged ones for the February storms. That is the single most common gutter failure we see in Thousand Oaks, Westlake Village, Agoura Hills, and Ojai, and it is not carelessness. It is national advice applied to a local tree.

## When to Schedule

1. **Late October or November, before the first real storm.** This clears the summer accumulation of grit, dust, and wind-blown debris.
2. **April, after the oaks have finished.** On a property with mature coast live oak, sycamore, or pine over the roofline, this is the visit that matters most and the one almost nobody books.
3. **After any wind event.** Santa Ana conditions can fill a gutter in a single night, and they do not wait for the calendar.

Homes with no significant tree cover over the roof can often run on one annual cleaning in the fall. Homes under a canopy generally cannot. We will tell you which one you are at the quote rather than selling you a schedule you do not need.

## Gutters and Wildfire Risk

Much of our service area sits in or beside the wildland urban interface. The National Fire Protection Association notes that embers and small flames are the main way most homes ignite in a wildfire, and its first-zone checklist begins with an instruction to "clean roofs and gutters of dead leaves, debris and pine needles that could catch embers." A gutter packed with dry oak litter through a Santa Ana event is a horizontal collection tray for airborne embers, positioned directly against the fascia and the eave. Clearing it is one of the cheapest hardening steps available to a homeowner.

## Pricing

We do not publish a flat gutter cleaning rate, and we would rather explain why than post a number we cannot stand behind. What actually moves a gutter price is:

- **Linear feet of gutter**, which is a function of the roof's footprint and not of the house's square footage
- **Single story versus two story**, which changes the ladder work and the time
- **Roof pitch and access**, including whether hardscape, slopes, or landscaping limit ladder placement
- **Existing gutter guards**, which have to come off and go back on
- **Debris load**, which is the difference between a maintained gutter and one that has not been touched in three years
