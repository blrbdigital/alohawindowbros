## Why Ventura's Coastal Climate Is Hard on Windows

Ventura sits on the Pacific between the mouths of the Ventura River and the Santa Clara River, so most of the city lives with marine air that inland cities never see. Here's what Ventura homeowners deal with:

- **Salt spray and marine layer:** ocean moisture carries dissolved salt that dries on glass as a hazy film and hardens if it is left for months.
- **Morning fog:** the marine layer condenses on cool glass overnight and evaporates by midday, leaving behind whatever it carried.
- **Santa Ana winds:** hot, dry winds that come down the Santa Clara River Valley in fall and winter coat exterior glass in fine dust, and ash in fire season.
- **Pollen:** coastal sage scrub, eucalyptus, and sycamore on the Ventura hillsides release heavy seasonal pollen.
- **Mineral-heavy local water:** covered in the next section, because it is the variable most guides to this city leave out.

## Ventura's Water Is the Hidden Variable

**Ventura imports no water.** According to the City of Ventura's 2024 drought resiliency grant application to the [US Bureau of Reclamation](https://www.usbr.gov/drought/docs/2024/DRP-041_City_of_San_Buenaventura_508.pdf), the city meets demand "exclusively with local water supplies": about 45% surface water from the Ventura River and Lake Casitas, and about 54% groundwater from city wells in three basins.

| Ventura supply | Share | What the city reports |
|---|---|---|
| Ventura River and Lake Casitas (surface water) | About 45% | The higher-quality water the city blends with |
| Mound, Oxnard Plain, and Santa Paula basins (groundwater) | About 54% | 1,200 to 1,600 mg/L total dissolved solids, sulfate often above 500 mg/L |
| Santa Paula Basin (east end of the city) | Part of the groundwater share | "Highly mineralized," averaging about 1,300 mg/L total dissolved solids |
| Recycled water | Small | From the city's Ventura Water Reclamation Facility |

The city blends its groundwater with surface water before delivery, so your tap is not running at 1,600 mg/L. But its raw groundwater sits above the 1,000 mg/L secondary standard the city itself cites, and that is more than half the supply. For windows, total dissolved solids (TDS) is the number that matters: TDS is everything left behind when water evaporates, and 1 mg/L is the same as 1 ppm. When sprinkler overspray dries on glass, those minerals stay as white spots. [Unger](https://usa.ungerglobal.com/blog/10-common-mistakes-in-pure-water-window-cleaning/) says rinse water above 10 ppm raises the risk of streaks and spots, which is why we never finish a Ventura job with tap water. Our [purified water window cleaning guide](/blog/purified-water-window-cleaning/) explains how the rinse system works.

Relief may come to the east end. The same application describes a 4.3-mile pipeline to the Calleguas Municipal Water District system, scheduled for November 2024 to May 2027, that would let Ventura take delivery of its 10,000 acre-foot per year State Water Project entitlement and blend it into Santa Paula Basin supply. Until then the practical advice holds: keep sprinklers off the glass, and do not rinse windows with tap water.

## Protecting Your Windows from Ventura's Elements

Between professional cleanings, Ventura homeowners can extend the time their windows stay clear:

- **Adjust sprinklers:** turn irrigation heads away from windows. It is the cheapest way to prevent mineral spots.
- **Skip the tap-water hose-down:** after a Santa Ana event it is tempting, but Ventura's water dries into spots that are harder to remove than the dust.
- **Keep landscaping trimmed:** plants touching windows trap moisture and cause staining.
- **Check weatherstripping:** coastal humidity wears window seals faster, so inspect them once a year.
