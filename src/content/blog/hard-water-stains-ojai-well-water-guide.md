## Why Ojai Water Is So Hard

Most Ventura County cities drink imported water. Ojai largely does not.

The Casitas Municipal Water District [2024 Annual Drinking Water Quality Report](https://www.casitaswater.org/files/f8eb38490/final.web.2025+CMWD-Ojai+Water+Quality+Report_2024+Data.pdf) for the Ojai Water System confirms that groundwater is pumped from the Ojai Valley Groundwater Basin through seven wells located in the town of Ojai, supplemented periodically with treated water from Casitas Municipal Water District. That local groundwater percolates through the valley's sedimentary rock, and it picks up calcium and magnesium the whole way down.

Imported water does not do that. It arrives already treated.

> "According to the Ventura County Public Works Agency, Ojai water averages 17 grains per gallon. In the parts of Ventura County served by State Water Project supply through the Metropolitan Water District, hardness runs as low as 5.2 grains per gallon. Ojai homeowners are cleaning up after roughly three times the mineral load."

The [Ventura County Public Works Agency](https://publicworks.venturacounty.gov/2022/06/06/eye-on-the-environment-conditions-for-water-conditioning-when-softeners-are-hard-on-the-environment/) groups Ojai with Fillmore, Santa Paula, and Ventura as the communities that rely on local water and therefore live with the county's highest hardness. Homes on private wells outside the Ojai Water System's service area can be harder still, because they get no blending with Lake Casitas surface water at all.

### Ojai vs the Rest of the County

Water hardness is measured in grains per gallon (gpg) or in milligrams per liter of calcium carbonate. To convert, divide mg/L by 17.1. The [U.S. Geological Survey](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/hardness-water) classifies anything above 121 mg/L as hard and anything above 180 mg/L as very hard.

| Water system | Hardness | Source of supply |
|---|---|---|
| Ojai Water System | About 17 gpg (roughly 290 mg/L), very hard | Seven local wells, Ojai Valley Groundwater Basin |
| Cal Water Westlake district | 146 ppm average, about 8.5 gpg | Purchased MWD surface water, no wells |
| Las Virgenes Municipal Water District | 146 mg/L average, about 8.5 gpg | Imported State Water Project |
| Ventura County areas on State Water Project | Up to 5.2 gpg | Metropolitan Water District |

This is the practical takeaway: a cleaning routine that keeps glass clear in Westlake Village is not enough in Ojai. Same sun, same sprinklers, double the minerals.

## How Hard Water Actually Damages Ojai Glass

A hard water stain is not dirt sitting on the surface. It is what stays behind after the water leaves.

When Ojai well water lands on a window, whether from sprinkler overspray, a hose rinse, or pool splash, the water evaporates in the valley sun and every dissolved mineral it carried stays on the glass. Repeat that a few hundred times over a summer and the deposit thickens into a visible white haze.

The damage then progresses in stages:

1. **Fresh spotting (0 to 60 days)** - minerals sit on the surface and rinse away easily
2. **Bonded deposits (2 to 12 months)** - the layer hardens and needs a mineral dissolver plus agitation
3. **Etching (12 months and beyond)** - minerals chemically bond with the silica in the glass, permanently clouding it

> "Once hard water has etched glass, no cleaning product on the market will restore it. The clouding is no longer on the window, it is in the window. At that point the choice is professional polishing, which recovers most but not all clarity, or replacing the pane."

Because Ojai water carries roughly double the mineral load of imported water, Ojai glass moves through those three stages faster. That is the entire argument for cleaning on a schedule here rather than waiting until the windows look bad.

## The Ojai Trap: Rinsing With Your Own Water

Here is the mistake Aloha Window Bros sees most often in the Ojai Valley.

A homeowner notices spotting and hoses the windows down. The spotting comes back worse. They hose them down again. It gets worse again.

The hose is the problem. Rinsing Ojai glass with Ojai well water does not remove minerals, it delivers a fresh coat of them, and then the valley sun bakes that coat onto the window. The homeowner is not failing to scrub hard enough. They are washing the glass with the exact substance that is staining it.

This is the same mechanism that clogs [window screens across the Ojai Valley](/blog/screen-cleaning-ojai-orchard-pollen-and-well-water-guide), except on a screen the scale hides inside the mesh where no squeegee reaches.

The fix is purified water. Aloha Window Bros filters the dissolved minerals out before the water ever touches the house, which does two things at once: it dissolves and lifts existing scale, and it dries leaving nothing behind at all. No minerals in, no minerals left over.

## Removing Hard Water Stains in Ojai

What works depends entirely on how long the deposits have been there.

| Deposit age | Method | Realistic outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Under 60 days | White vinegar solution or purified water wash | Full removal |
| 2 to 6 months | Commercial mineral dissolver, agitation, purified rinse | 90 to 100% removal |
| 6 to 12 months | Professional polishing compound and buffing | 70 to 95% removal |
| Over 12 months | Restoration attempt, or replacement quote | Variable, often permanent |

For fresh spotting, a 1:1 mix of distilled white vinegar and warm water, left to dwell five to ten minutes and never allowed to dry on the glass, will handle light deposits. Note the word distilled. Mixing your cleaning solution with hard tap water in Ojai partially defeats it before you start.

What not to do, in any city but especially here:

- **Do not use steel wool or scouring pads.** They will scratch the glass permanently, and the scratches hold minerals better than smooth glass does.
- **Do not use ammonia glass cleaner** on mineral deposits. It does nothing to calcium and it streaks.
- **Do not clean in direct sun.** In the Ojai summer, solutions flash off before they can work.
- **Do not let any solution dry on the pane.** That is how you create a second problem on top of the first.

## Prevention Is Far Cheaper Than Restoration

Restoration on a badly spotted Ojai home runs $200 to $500. Replacing a single etched pane runs $300 to $800. Prevention costs a fraction of either.

1. **Aim your sprinklers away from the house.** Sprinkler overspray is the single largest source of hard water stains on Ojai windows. Rotate the heads or swap spray heads for drip in beds along the walls.
2. **Water before sunrise.** Water that sits on glass in full Ojai sun evaporates fast and deposits everything it carried. Cooler air gives you a slower, gentler dry.
3. **Never hose down the windows.** In Ojai this makes it worse, not better.
4. **Clean on a schedule, not on appearance.** By the time hard water haze is obvious, it is already bonded.
5. **Consider a hydrophobic glass sealant.** It causes water to bead and roll off rather than evaporate in place, buying real time between cleanings.

Because of the hardness, Aloha Window Bros recommends professional cleaning every three to four months for Ojai homes with sprinklers near the glass, versus every four to six months for homes on imported water elsewhere in the county. Quarterly service keeps deposits in the "rinses right off" stage and never lets them reach the etching stage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How hard is the water in Ojai?

Ojai water averages about 17 grains per gallon, or roughly 290 mg/L of calcium carbonate, according to the Ventura County Public Works Agency. The U.S. Geological Survey classifies anything above 180 mg/L as very hard. For comparison, the imported State Water Project water serving cities like Westlake Village runs about 8.5 grains per gallon, and some Ventura County areas on State Water Project supply run as low as 5.2.

### Why does Ojai have harder water than Thousand Oaks or Westlake Village?

Because Ojai drinks local groundwater and they do not. The Casitas Municipal Water District Ojai Water System pumps from seven wells in the Ojai Valley Groundwater Basin, and that groundwater picks up calcium and magnesium as it moves through the valley's rock. Westlake Village and Thousand Oaks are supplied with imported surface water that arrives already treated and considerably softer.

### Can I remove hard water stains from my Ojai windows myself?

Fresh spotting, under about 60 days old, usually comes off with a 1:1 distilled white vinegar and warm water solution. Use distilled water in the mix, because making the solution with Ojai tap water adds minerals back. Deposits older than roughly six months typically need professional polishing compounds, and deposits past 12 months may have etched the glass permanently.

### Will a home water softener fix my window stains?

It helps, but only for water that comes through the house plumbing. Most window staining comes from irrigation, and irrigation lines are usually plumbed upstream of the softener, so the sprinklers keep spraying untreated hard water regardless. Redirecting sprinkler overspray away from the glass does more for your windows than a softener does.

### Are hard water stains on Ojai windows permanent?

Not if you catch them. Deposits under 12 months old can usually be fully or nearly fully restored. Past 12 months, minerals begin bonding chemically with the silica in the glass and the clouding becomes etching, which cannot be cleaned off.
