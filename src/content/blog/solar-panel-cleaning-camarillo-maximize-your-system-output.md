## Key takeaways

- **Typical loss is modest.** California panels lost 7.4% over a 145-day dry spell, under 0.05% a day, in UC San Diego's study. NREL puts annual soiling losses at up to 7% in parts of the United States.
- **The exceptions are Camarillo's everyday conditions.** Bird droppings, low-tilt arrays, and homes downwind of fields or highways are where cleaning pays.

## How Much Does Dirt Actually Cost a Camarillo Solar System?

Less than most cleaning ads claim, and that is worth knowing before you pay anyone. Engineers at [UC San Diego](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/cleaning_solar_panels_often_not_worth_the_cost_engineers_at_uc_san_diego_fi) analyzed 186 residential and commercial sites between the Bay Area and the Mexican border. Panels that went 145 days without rain or cleaning lost 7.4% of their efficiency, and for a typical 5 kW home system, washing halfway through that dry summer would have gained about $20 of electricity.

| Setting | Soiling loss | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California homes, 145 dry days | 7.4% total, under 0.05% a day | UC San Diego |
| Parts of the United States | Up to 7% a year | NREL |
| Bird droppings on a panel | Blocks essentially all light where it sits | UC San Diego |

The same study names the cases where cleaning is worth it: bird droppings, which block light and do not rain off, very flat arrays that rain cannot rinse, and sites downwind of highways, factories, or farm fields. The [National Renewable Energy Laboratory](https://www.nlr.gov/news/detail/features/2021/scientists-studying-solar-try-solving-a-dusty-problem) reports the same pattern at a larger scale, with losses of up to 7% in parts of the United States and up to 50% in the Middle East.

## Why Camarillo Roofs Are the Exception

Camarillo sits on the edge of the Oxnard Plain, one of California's most productive farming regions, and a short drive from the coast. That puts several of the study's exceptions on the same roof:

- **Farm dust:** tilled strawberry and row-crop fields release fine soil that drifts onto nearby roofs, heaviest on homes that back onto fields.
- **Road dust:** homes near the 101 collect traffic grime that clings to glass.
- **Salt film:** onshore marine air carries salt inland, and salt holds moisture, so it turns dust into a sticky film.
- **Birds:** orchards and open fields support large bird populations, and a single dropping shades the cells under it.
- **Wildfire ash:** fire seasons leave fine ash on horizontal surfaces for weeks.

| Camarillo situation | Main soiling source | Suggested interval |
|---|---|---|
| Home backing onto farm fields | Farm dust, birds | Every 3 to 4 months, or when output drops |
| Near the 101 or a busy arterial | Road grime | Every 4 to 6 months |
| Typical neighborhood, steep roof | Light dust, pollen | Every 6 months |
| Low-tilt or nearly flat array | Dust that rain cannot rinse | Inspect quarterly, clean as needed |
| After a fire or major Santa Ana wind event | Ash and dust | Inspect within a few weeks |

These intervals follow the guidance on our [solar panel cleaning service page](/services/solar-panel-cleaning/): every 4 to 6 months inland, sooner where the glass and your monitoring app say so.

## Why Hose Water Spots Camarillo Panels

Part of Camarillo is served by the Camrosa Water District, and its water is a blend. According to Camrosa's [2024 water quality report](https://www.camrosa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CCR2024.pdf), about 55% of its 2024 supply came from its own wells and the rest was imported through the Calleguas Municipal Water District. The imported water measured **148 ppm** total hardness, about 8.7 grains per gallon. The district's wells measured **375 to 540 ppm**, about 22 to 32 grains per gallon. Some well water is blended with imported water and some is pumped directly into the system, so what comes out of a Camarillo hose depends on where you live.

Either way, water that hard leaves minerals behind when it dries on hot glass. Panel makers agree on the principle even where they differ on details: REC's manual calls for deionized water, and First Solar says reverse osmosis water is best. Our [rooftop solar cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-solar-panels-on-roof/) compares what REC, Qcells, and First Solar each allow, including where they disagree on soap and brushes.

| Factor | Professional cleaning | DIY with a garden hose |
|---|---|---|
| Rinse water | Purified, so nothing is left to dry on the glass | Camarillo tap water, which can be very hard |
| Salt and film | Loosened with a soft brush, then rinsed | Often redistributed rather than removed |
| Roof safety | Insured crew with fall protection | Ladder and roof risk, plus live electrical equipment |
| Manufacturer rules | No pressure washers, no abrasives, no harsh chemicals | Easy to break a rule without knowing it |
