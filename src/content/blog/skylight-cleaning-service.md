**A skylight cleaning service washes the exterior pane, the interior pane, the frame and flashing perimeter, and any screen or track, using a method chosen for the glazing material rather than a single all-purpose spray.** That last part is the whole job. Glass skylights and acrylic or polycarbonate domes take opposite instructions from their own manufacturers, and the household glass cleaner most people reach for is explicitly prohibited on plastic. Architectural Specialties, whose dome skylight maintenance manual is quoted below, writes it in capitals: "DO NOT USE Windex or other household cleaners." Get the material wrong once and the damage is permanent, because a crazed or scratched acrylic dome cannot be polished back to clear.

The second half of the job is access. Under federal safety rules a skylight is not a surface at all. It is a hole in the roof.

## Key takeaways

- **Identify the material first.** Glass tolerates a mild non-abrasive glass cleaner. Acrylic and polycarbonate domes do not, and solvent damage is irreversible.
- **Windex is banned on domes by the manufacturer**, along with acetone, gasoline, benzene, and lacquer thinner ([Architectural Specialties](https://archsp.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Skylight-Maintenance.pdf)).
- **Never pressure wash a skylight.** The same manual warns it can dislodge gaskets, which turns a cleaning job into a leak.
- **OSHA classes a skylight as a hole**, not a walking surface, and requires fall protection above 4 feet ([29 CFR 1910.28(b)(3)(i)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.28)).
- **Most skylights can be cleaned from the ground.** VELUX tells its own customers that most window cleaning companies carry telescoping brushes that reach the exterior without anyone getting on the roof.
- **There is no honest flat per-skylight rate.** Roof pitch, access route, glazing, and unit count move it too far to average.

## What actually makes a skylight look hazy

Homeowners rarely notice a dirty skylight. They notice that the room went dim, or that the overhead view turned milky. In coastal Ventura County and Santa Barbara County, salt aerosol builds a film that returns faster than it does on a sheltered inland elevation. In the foothills around Thousand Oaks, Simi Valley, and Ojai, Santa Ana and Sundowner winds lay down dry dust that the first rain turns into streaks running toward the frame.

Three of those conditions are removable residue. Two are not:

- **Removable:** salt film, windblown dust and pollen, organic buildup at the perimeter, bird residue, and mineral spotting from sprinkler overspray.
- **Not removable by cleaning:** fine scratching from a previous abrasive pad or a scraper, chemical crazing on plastic, condensation trapped between insulated glass panes, and a failed seal or coating.

A crew's first job is telling those apart before it wets anything, because cleaning a failed seal harder does not fix it. VELUX puts the diagnostic plainly in its own care guidance: if streaks of condensation remain between the panes after cleaning, that points to sealant failure and replacement, not a dirtier skylight.

## The material question, and why the two manufacturers contradict each other

This is the part almost no cleaning guide covers, and it is the reason a generic answer is worse than no answer.

| Glazing | How to tell from the ground | Manufacturer approved method | Explicitly prohibited | Failure mode if you get it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat glass** (most modern units) | Flat or gently sloped plane, set flush in a metal or wood curb frame | Soak with clean water and soap, then a mild non-abrasive glass cleaner with a soft brush, removed at once with a squeegee or lint free cloth | Metal scrapers, blades, or knives across large areas; abrasive pads; letting metal tool parts touch the glass | Permanent scratching, and scrape damage is **not covered by the VELUX warranty** |
| **Acrylic or polycarbonate dome** (common on older homes and additions) | Raised bubble or pyramid profile, softer edges, often slightly yellowed with age | Liquid dish detergent and water only, with a soft cloth, sponge, or chamois; approved plastic polish for marks | **Window cleaning sprays such as Windex**, kitchen scouring compounds, acetone, gasoline, benzene, carbon tetrachloride, lacquer thinner | Crazing and clouding that cannot be reversed, plus scratching that scatters light so the dome reads hazy even when clean |
| **Either, unknown age or coating** | Manufacturer marking on the frame, installation paperwork, or a tint or mirrored cast in sunlight | Treat as the more sensitive of the two: water, mild detergent, soft cloth | Anything solvent based, anything abrasive, and pressure washing | Coating strip on low emissivity or tinted glass, which cannot be reapplied on site |

Two rules apply to every row. **Do not pressure wash any skylight**, because force near the perimeter can dislodge gaskets and push water past the seal, and **do not stand or crawl directly on the glazing**. Both come from the same Architectural Specialties manual.

If you are unsure what you have, the conservative choice is the acrylic column. Water and mild detergent will not harm glass. A solvent will destroy plastic.

## How crews reach a skylight without getting on the roof

Access decides the method, and it is where the risk actually sits. Federal rule 29 CFR 1910.28(b)(3)(i) requires that a worker be protected from falling through **any hole, including skylights, that is 4 feet or more above a lower level**, by covers, guardrails, travel restraint, or a personal fall arrest system. That is a workplace standard written for employers rather than a rule aimed at homeowners, but the classification is the useful part: to a safety regulator, a skylight is an opening a person can fall through, not a place to put weight.

The good news is that most residential skylights never require anyone on the roof. VELUX tells its own customers that most window cleaning companies carry telescoping brushes and squeegees that reach the exterior from the ground, and asks owners only to include skylights in the window count they give the company.

| Access method | Suits | Controls required | Where it stops working |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground based purified water pole | Reachable exterior panes and domes on most single and two story homes | Stable footing, hose routing away from walkways, overhead hazard check | Trees, deep setbacks, roof geometry, pole weight, and any overhead power line |
| Controlled ladder placement | Lower roof sections with straightforward approach | Secured ladder on a stable base, no stepping onto the glazing | Steep pitch, tile, moss, wet or gritty surfaces |
| Roof access with fall protection | Units that genuinely cannot be reached otherwise | Trained personnel, anchor assessment, travel path plan, edge exposure control | Any roof the crew cannot verify will carry the load |

The Federation of Window Cleaners, a trade body rather than a competitor, publishes [safety guidance for water fed pole systems](https://f-w-c.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Safety-in-Window-Cleaning-using-Waterfed-Pole-Systems.pdf) warning that a raised carbon pole near overhead power lines carries a severe electrocution risk, and advising operators to keep hands below shoulder height and alternate arms on long work. The same principles behind [cleaning high windows safely](/blog/window-cleaning-for-high-windows/) apply here, with two additions specific to skylights: the glazing is fragile, and it is an opening.

## Why purified water matters more on a skylight than on a wall window

A skylight sits at a shallow angle facing straight up, so rinse water sheets off slowly and evaporates in place rather than running off the pane. Anything dissolved in that water dries where it sat.

That is why professional exterior work uses purified water, which carries no dissolved minerals to leave behind. On a vertical window a mineral rich rinse leaves faint spotting. On an upward facing skylight it leaves the full deposit, in the middle of the pane, where the sun then bakes it. If your skylight already carries that kind of spotting from years of sprinkler overspray or tap water rinsing, the underlying chemistry is the same one covered in our guide to [removing hard water spots from windows](/blog/how-to-remove-hard-water-spots-from-windows/), and the [purified water method](/blog/purified-water-window-cleaning/) is what prevents it recurring.

What we can tell you is the shape of the bill it attaches to. Skylights are quoted on top of that after an on site look, and several accessible units often cost less together than one awkward one alone.

Five things move the number:

1. **Access route.** A ground reachable dome and a roof access job with fall protection are different categories of work, not different sizes of the same job.
2. **Unit count and grouping.** Units that share one setup are efficient. Units scattered across three roof planes are three setups.
3. **Interior as well as exterior.** Interior work adds furniture protection, drip control, and detailing around trim.
4. **Condition and material.** Heavy mineral deposits, a neglected perimeter, or delicate acrylic all slow the process down.
5. **Screens, tracks, and frames.** Ask whether removal, washing, drying, and reinstallation are included or priced separately, because "skylight cleaning" can otherwise mean the visible pane only.

## When cleaning is the wrong answer

A cleaning technician should not promise to fix a seal with a cleaning solution. Ask for a different trade when you see any of these:

- Cracked, crazed, or yellowed plastic that does not change appearance when wet
- Moisture or fogging **between** the panes of an insulated unit
- Damaged or lifted flashing at the perimeter
- Staining that remains after the surface is demonstrably clean
- Any soft spot or movement in the surrounding roof deck

The right outcome in those cases is a referral to a roofer, glazier, or the skylight manufacturer, and a quote for the units that genuinely just need washing.
