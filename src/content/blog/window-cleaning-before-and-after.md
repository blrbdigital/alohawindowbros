## Turn Clean Glass Into Credible Local Proof

A before-and-after gallery earns trust when it documents a repeatable result, not when it collects attractive finished photos. Start with the same camera angle before and after service. Keep the pane boundaries, surrounding frame, and lighting as comparable as possible so viewers can identify the change without guessing whether the photograph created it.

Label the problem in plain language. Use terms such as coastal haze, mineral spotting, construction residue, screen dust, frame buildup, sill debris, or organic growth when the image supports that description. Don't call a surface “restored” if the work was routine cleaning, and don't promise like-new glass when long-term mineral damage has etched the surface.

<a id="a-practical-evidence-standard"></a>
### A practical evidence standard

- **Show the complete zone:** Include screens, frames, tracks, and sills when they're part of the quoted service.
- **Identify the method:** State whether the crew used squeegees, purified water-fed poles, pressurized screen tools, restoration treatment, or specialized access equipment.
- **Explain access:** Note whether the work involved high glass, skylights, multi-story panes, ladders, platforms, or ground-level poles.
- **State location and scope:** Name the city or property type, then clarify whether the images show residential, commercial, storefront, HOA, listing-prep, or post-construction work.
- **Separate estimate from price:** Provide a range only when it comes from a defined assessment or published provider information. Otherwise, invite an on-site or virtual quote.
- **Add a trust signal:** Pair the image with a verifiable review, insurance information, licensing detail where applicable, or a satisfaction and rework policy.

APPA's facilities guidance says the best way to evaluate cleaning effectiveness is to measure conditions before and after cleaning, rather than relying on appearance alone. Its principle supports a stronger operational record: document film, streaking, mineral spotting, dust, and debris before service, then compare the result with a defined acceptance standard. That approach helps commercial properties, HOAs, and listing-preparation teams justify recurring maintenance because the record shows whether the service materially improved the glass and surrounding surfaces.

Safety evidence deserves equal weight. In Great Britain, falls from height killed **35 workers across all industries in 2024/25**, and falls from height accounted for **28% of workplace deaths over the preceding five years**, according to the [parliamentary safety summary cited by IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/window-washing/6458/). The same source records **eight window-cleaning fall incidents**, five fatal, during that period. Those figures explain why a high-window gallery should show safer access planning, water-fed poles, fall protection, or other appropriate controls instead of presenting height as a visual afterthought.

Homeowners and property managers should compare evidence, not image volume. Ask whether the provider serves your area, defines the included surfaces, explains difficult deposits, and gives you a way to review the work before the crew leaves.
