## Where Your Home Lands Inside Its Band

A band is a range because two houses on the same street can take very different amounts of crew time. This is how the same four-in-one clean moves inside a band, with no change to the price list:

| What the crew finds | Where the quote lands | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Single story, standard windows, screens in good shape, cleaned in the last three to four months | Low end of your city's band | Light soil comes off in one pass |
| Two stories, a wall of glass, or many sliding doors | Middle of the band | Upper glass needs water-fed pole work or ladder setup at every window |
| Divided-lite panes, heavy sprinkler spotting, or screens not washed in over a year | Top of the band | Every small pane is cleaned on its own, and loaded screens take longer than glass |
| Glass that stays cloudy wet or dry | Separate restoration quote, $200 to $500 | The minerals have etched the glass, so it needs polishing, not washing |

Regular service is priced at the low end of a band. Neglected glass is often priced at the high end of the next band up, because salt and mineral deposits build on each other between visits. ## Estimate Your Own Home in Five Minutes

Online price calculators ask for square footage, which is the one number that predicts the least. A 2,000 square foot two-story with 34 windows takes longer than a 2,400 square foot ranch with 18. Walk the house once and note these five things instead:

1. **Count windows the way a crew does.** Count every window unit and every sliding glass door. Note French or divided-lite windows separately, because each small pane is cleaned on its own. Most homes we quote have 18 to 34 windows.
2. **Mark anything above the first floor.** Second-story windows, tall entry glass, clerestories and skylights each add setup time.
3. **Count the screens.** Note how many there are and whether they have been washed in the last year. Screens are the part of the job most often left out of cheap quotes.
4. **Run the wet test on your worst pane.** Wet a spotted area and look again. Haze that changes when wet is surface mineral and comes off in a normal clean. Haze that looks the same wet and dry is etched glass, and that is restoration.
5. **Note the last professional clean.** Within three to four months points to the low end of your band. More than a year points to the top.

Send those five answers and a few photos with your request and you will get a far tighter number than any calculator gives. Our [window washing service page](/services/window-washing/) lists exactly what a visit includes.

## What California Law Puts Under Every Quote

Every legitimate quote in this state has to cover costs the law sets, before the company earns anything.

| Cost | What the rule says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wages | Minimum wage is **$16.90 an hour** for all employers from January 1, 2026, unless a higher industry or local minimum applies | [California Department of Industrial Relations](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_minimumwage.htm) |
| Workers' compensation | California employers "are required by law to have workers' compensation insurance, even if they have only one employee" | [California Department of Industrial Relations](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dwc/employer.htm) |

Here is what that floor means in practice. As an illustration, not our cost sheet: two technicians on a three-hour job cost at least **$101.40 in wages alone** at the 2026 minimum (2 x 3 hours x $16.90), before workers' compensation, payroll taxes, liability insurance, fuel, purified-water resin and drive time. A whole-house quote far below the local band is not automatically dishonest, but it usually leaves something out: interior glass, screens, or insurance. Ask which one before you book. ## Per Window, Per Pane or Flat Rate

Quotes in this trade come in three forms, and each one can be fair if the written scope is clear.

- **Per window.** Simple, but a double-hung window has two sashes, and one company counts it as one window while another counts two panes.
- **Per pane.** A closer match to labor on divided-lite glass, as long as the company defines a pane before quoting.
- **Flat rate.** One total for the whole house after someone has counted the glass. This is how Aloha Window Bros quotes, because a firm number after an on-site or video look is the only one that will not move on the day.

You can still translate between them. Across the 18 to 34 window homes we typically quote, our $150 to $475 band works out to roughly **$6 to $20 per window**. That figure is arithmetic after the fact, not a rate we charge. It is also why a per-window price under $8 deserves one question: does it include screens, frames and sills, or exterior glass only? <a id="why-the-same-house-costs-more-in-some-cities"></a>
## Why the Same House Costs More in Some Cities

Two things move the band between cities: drive time and what is on the glass. Drive time is simple. Water takes a little more explaining.

A spot-free finish depends on rinsing with purified water. Unger, the equipment maker, says that once rinse water rises above **10 ppm** of total dissolved solids it "may not be as effective in producing streak-free and spot-free results" ([Unger](https://usa.ungerglobal.com/blog/10-common-mistakes-in-pure-water-window-cleaning/)). Local tap water starts far above that. California American Water reports **297 ppm** of total dissolved solids for its main Thousand Oaks and Newbury Park supply ([Cal Am](https://www.amwater.com/ccr/thousandoaks.pdf)), and the City of Ventura's groundwater runs **1,200 to 1,600 mg/L** before blending ([City of Ventura](https://www.usbr.gov/drought/docs/2024/DRP-041_City_of_San_Buenaventura_508.pdf)). The harder the water, the more filtration media each job uses, and the heavier the mineral spotting a crew has to remove first. Our guide to [purified water window cleaning](/blog/purified-water-window-cleaning/) explains the rinse itself.

Coastal homes add a second load: salt film on ocean-facing glass. Together with agricultural dust, that is why Oxnard's band tops out at $450 rather than $425.

<a id="how-to-compare-quotes"></a>
## How to Compare Quotes

Compare the scope first and the price second. A reliable quote states in writing:

- **Every surface:** interior and exterior glass, screens, frames, sills and tracks, glass doors and skylights.
- **Access:** which windows need ladders or a water-fed pole.
- **Condition:** whether hard water spotting is part of the clean or a separate restoration quote.
- **The rinse:** purified water on the final pass, or tap water.
- **Insurance:** a certificate of liability coverage, and workers' compensation if the company has employees.
- **The total:** a firm price after an on-site or video look, with no screen, travel or access charges added on the day.

> **Ask this directly:** "Does your total include screens, tracks, sills and every second-story window?"
