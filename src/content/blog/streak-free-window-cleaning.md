Most streaks aren't caused by a bad spray or a worn-out squeegee. They usually come from **mineral-heavy water, glass that dries too quickly, or dirt moving back onto the pane from the frame, track, sill, or screen**. A better cleaner can help, but it can't compensate for contaminated rinse water or a hot window in direct sun.

After years of working on coastal and inland homes, the reliable approach is less about chasing a perfect recipe and more about controlling the whole job. Prepare the window system, match the method to the soil, check water quality, and give the solution enough working time to be removed cleanly. That's the foundation of **streak-free window cleaning** that lasts beyond the first change in sunlight.

## Why Windows Streak Even When You Do Everything Right

The common assumption is that streaks mean you chose the wrong cleaner or failed to use the squeegee correctly. Those things can matter, but they're often secondary. Three less obvious causes create most of the repeat problems: **dissolved minerals, premature drying, and surrounding contamination**.

![A diagram illustrating three common reasons why window glass might streak despite proper cleaning efforts.](https://cdnimg.co/8387fe5a-1497-428d-a3af-57b68fc0bac4/af87f75b-09ba-4f53-a210-fe291eb78455/streak-free-window-cleaning-window-streaks.jpg)

<a id="mineral-residue-is-already-in-the-rinse-water"></a>
### Mineral residue is already in the rinse water

Tap water can look perfectly clear while carrying calcium and magnesium. The [National Window Cleaning Authority's explanation of pure-water cleaning](https://nationalwindowcleaningauthority.com/pure-water-window-cleaning/) notes that water purity is central to the result, with professional workflows commonly targeting **0 to 10 ppm TDS**, and **0 to 5 ppm** considered optimal. At that level, the water dries without depositing the same mineral film.

This is especially noticeable in hard-water areas. The same guidance cites U.S. Geological Survey information stating that **85% of American homes have hard water** containing dissolved calcium and magnesium. Coastal humidity, sprinkler overspray, and salt-laden air can make the residue more obvious, but the mechanism is straightforward: water evaporates, minerals remain.

<a id="heat-turns-a-workable-job-into-a-race"></a>
### Heat turns a workable job into a race

A pane in direct sun can dry the cleaning solution before the squeegee or cloth has removed the suspended soil. South-facing glass in winter can create the same problem as summer heat, especially when the outdoor air feels cool but the surface is warm. The result looks like a cleaner problem, but the issue is lost working time.

<a id="the-glass-isnt-the-whole-window"></a>
### The glass isn't the whole window

Dust sits in tracks, pollen clings to screens, and dirty sills hold contaminated water. Once the glass looks clean, movement, wind, or a small drip can carry that material back over the pane. [Kärcher's window-cleaning guidance](https://www.kaercher.com/us/home-garden/know-how/cleaning-windows.html) also recommends avoiding direct sun and cleaning surrounding parts of the window before the pane.

> **Working rule:** If the glass looks perfect for a few minutes and then develops marks, inspect the water, drying conditions, and frame before changing products.

<a id="prepping-the-whole-window-system-before-you-touch-the-glass"></a>
## Prepping the Whole Window System Before You Touch the Glass

A clean pane can be spoiled by a dirty track faster than expected. I start with the dry parts of the assembly because wetting grit turns loose dust into slurry, and slurry is much harder to control.

<a id="start-dry-then-remove-the-loose-contamination"></a>
### Start dry, then remove the loose contamination

Use a soft detailing brush or a vacuum with a narrow attachment on the frame, track, sill, seals, and drainage openings. Pay particular attention to the lower corners, where debris collects and later mixes with rinse water. Don't force a brush into brittle seals, and don't block weep holes with cloth or cleaner.

Remove screens before mixing solution when the design allows it. Brush or rinse them outside, let them drain, and set them somewhere clean while you work. A screen full of pollen can shed particles onto freshly washed glass, especially when air moves through the opening.

![A three-step infographic showing how to prep windows for cleaning by brushing tracks, wiping seals, and protecting surroundings.](https://cdnimg.co/8387fe5a-1497-428d-a3af-57b68fc0bac4/31579328-f3b9-4549-a5da-3b75954a658d/streak-free-window-cleaning-window-prep.jpg)

<a id="choose-the-conditions-before-choosing-the-cleaner"></a>
### Choose the conditions before choosing the cleaner

Work on the shaded side of the building whenever possible. Avoid direct sun, strong wind, and glass that feels hot to the touch. A cool, overcast period gives the solution time to suspend grime instead of flashing dry into a haze.

For indoor work, indirect light is useful because it reveals remaining film without heating the pane. Protect the sill and floor before wetting the glass, particularly around wood trim or painted surfaces.

<a id="stage-the-tools-in-the-order-youll-use-them"></a>
### Stage the tools in the order you'll use them

Keep the bucket, washer or applicator, squeegee, edge cloth, and a second dry microfiber within reach. One cloth should handle general moisture, while another stays clean for the perimeter. If you're working inside, place a towel below the pane before the first wet pass.

This preparation takes only a short time, but it prevents repeated trips, dirty tool changes, and frame drip-back. It treats the window as a system rather than a sheet of glass.

<a id="eco-friendly-solutions-that-actually-beat-streaks"></a>
## Eco-Friendly Solutions That Actually Beat Streaks

Eco-friendly cleaning doesn't mean using the same mixture for every pane. Vinegar, a small amount of dish soap, and purified water solve different problems, and the wrong match creates residue or leaves grease behind.

For ordinary interior film, a **1:1 mixture of white vinegar and distilled water** is a practical option. It handles light mineral residue and general glass film without relying on a heavy detergent load. The smell fades, but repeated or careless application around sealed wood can be a poor trade-off, so apply a light mist and keep the surrounding trim controlled.

Kitchen glass needs more degreasing power. Use **one drop of plant-based dish soap per gallon of distilled water**, then remove the solution thoroughly. Soap cuts cooking oils well, but the surfactant itself can leave haze if you don't rinse or wipe with clean water.

Purified water is different from either mixture. It's an excellent final rinse because low-mineral water can dry without the calcium and magnesium residue associated with spotting. It won't dissolve a greasy fingerprint film by itself, so it works best as an exterior cleaning method or as the final stage after an interior cleaner.

| Solution | Mix Ratio | Best For | Rinse Needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| White vinegar and distilled water | 1:1 | Light interior film and mineral residue | Clean-water wipe recommended |
| Plant-based dish soap and distilled water | One drop per gallon | Greasy kitchen panes | Yes, rinse thoroughly |
| Purified water | Use as supplied, verify low TDS | Exterior glass and final rinsing | No detergent rinse required |

For a deeper look at how soap choice affects residue and glide, see this guide to [window-washing soap](https://alohawindowbros.com/blog/window-washing-soap/). The practical choice depends on the room, the soil, and your water. Use vinegar for light interior film, diluted soap for grease, and purified water when mineral spotting is the problem or when you need a residue-free exterior finish.

<a id="interior-and-exterior-pane-techniques-that-work"></a>
## Interior and Exterior Pane Techniques That Work

Interior and exterior glass should be treated as separate jobs. Indoor panes need tighter moisture control because drips have nowhere to go, while exterior panes can usually be scrubbed and rinsed more generously.

![An infographic illustrating effective techniques for cleaning interior and exterior window panes for a streak-free finish.](https://cdnimg.co/8387fe5a-1497-428d-a3af-57b68fc0bac4/47000baa-2af4-493b-a255-12494440f128/streak-free-window-cleaning-window-tips.jpg)

<a id="interior-glass-needs-a-controlled-wet-edge"></a>
### Interior glass needs a controlled wet edge

Work in low, indirect light and mist the solution lightly onto the pane rather than soaking the cloth. Start at the upper corner, pull the squeegee across in a continuous motion, then continue across the window with overlapping passes. Wipe the rubber after each stroke so dirty water doesn't become a new line.

Keep moving from left to right. That rhythm gives you a consistent overlap and makes missed areas easier to spot. Avoid flooding the upper frame, because water held there can drip back after the pane appears finished.

Use a tight, lint-free microfiber around the perimeter before the edge bead creeps inward. A [proper window-washing squeegee technique](https://alohawindowbros.com/blog/window-washing-squeegee/) depends as much on blade condition and wiping discipline as on the pull itself.

<a id="exterior-glass-benefits-from-a-complete-scrub-and-rinse"></a>
### Exterior glass benefits from a complete scrub and rinse

For ground-floor exterior panes, scrub from the top down and finish with a pass that follows the lower frame. Use enough water to loosen grit, but don't press a hard, dirty pad into the glass. Exterior debris can contain fine particles that scratch when dragged dry.

Cold glass and hot solution can also produce an uneven film, so allow the surface and solution to work together instead of applying a very warm mix to a chilled pane. On textured, patterned, or frosted glass, a squeegee may catch in the surface. Use a clean microfiber or a suitable soft applicator instead, changing cloths as they load.

A squeegee is efficient on smooth glass, but it isn't automatically the right tool for every surface.



The final check should happen from more than one angle. If a mark moves when you change viewing position, it may be remaining moisture or film. If it stays fixed, inspect the glass, coating, or frame for a separate source.

## Purified-Water Pole Use for High and Awkward Glass

A water-fed pole succeeds because of water quality, not pole length alone. The system combines **water with very low dissolved solids**, a brush head, hose, and meter. A [purified-water window cleaning system](https://alohawindowbros.com/blog/purified-water-window-cleaning/) removes much of the mineral load that leaves spots as tap water dries.

Tap water can still carry enough minerals to mark glass, especially on sunny elevations where droplets evaporate quickly. An [industry technical guide to reverse-osmosis and water-fed pole cleaning](https://www.binx.ca/tech/reverse-osmosis-water-fed-pole-window-cleaning-us20120325733/) explains that RO removes roughly **95 to 99% of dissolved solids** before DI polishing. Check the meter during work. If the reading rises, the final rinse may start leaving the spotting the system was meant to prevent.

<a id="the-workflow-matters-more-than-the-gadget"></a>
### The workflow matters more than the gadget

Connect the RO or DI equipment, check the TDS meter, and purge the hose until the output reaches the intended range. The practical target is **0 to 10 ppm**. Results become less reliable above **10 ppm**, according to the technical guidance from Binx.

Work from the top of the pane downward. Agitate the glass thoroughly with the brush, then rinse long enough for the water to carry loosened soil toward the sill. Keep the rinse flowing over the surface instead of flicking small contaminated droplets onto areas already cleaned. Frames, tracks, and sills also need attention, because dirty runoff can re-contaminate the lower edge.

| TDS Reading (ppm) | Water Source | Streak Risk | Recommended Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Purified or DI-polished water | Lowest | Final rinse and routine pole work |
| 5 to 10 | Purified system output | Low when monitored | Exterior cleaning and high glass |
| Above 10 | Water with insufficient purification | Results become less reliable | Recheck filters and purify further |

A water-fed pole can keep the operator on the ground for reaches up to about **70 feet**, as described in the [history and development of purified-water window cleaning](https://hi-pointscaffolding.co.uk/blog/a-brief-history-of-window-cleaning/). That reduces ladder use, but it does not remove every hazard. Keep both feet on a stable surface, avoid overreaching, and stop when the pole becomes difficult to control.

<a id="know-when-the-pole-is-the-wrong-tool"></a>
### Know when the pole is the wrong tool

Heavy mineral buildup on frames, painted sills, cracked seals, and delicate surfaces often needs targeted hand work. Pure water handles routine soil and residue-free rinsing well, but baked-on deposits and damaged materials require a different method. For awkward glass with fragile surrounds, careful hand cleaning may be safer than forcing a pole into position.

## Troubleshooting the Streaks You Still Get

Persistent marks are easier to fix when you identify the symptom before changing the entire method.

![A troubleshooting chart explaining common causes and fixes for persistent streaks and spots on cleaned windows.](https://cdnimg.co/8387fe5a-1497-428d-a3af-57b68fc0bac4/2ed14314-7bb0-4d85-9186-2961c976a714/streak-free-window-cleaning-troubleshooting-guide.jpg)

<a id="white-spots-and-halos"></a>
### White spots and halos

White rings usually point to mineral-bearing water drying on the pane. Switch to distilled or purified water for the final rinse, and remove edge moisture before it sheets inward. If deposits are already established, a mild vinegar-water treatment can help, but test near the edge first and keep acidic solution away from vulnerable trim.

<a id="haze-that-appears-in-sun"></a>
### Haze that appears in sun

If the pane looks clear in shade but hazy after sunlight hits it, the solution probably dried before removal. Move to the shaded side, reduce the amount of solution, and cool the surface with a damp pre-wipe when appropriate. Work smaller areas instead of trying to wet the entire window at once.

<a id="drips-returning-from-the-frame"></a>
### Drips returning from the frame

A clean pane with new marks along the bottom often has contaminated water in the track. Vacuum or brush the track before cleaning, then hold a dry cloth along the lower edge during the final pass. Don't assume the glass caused the line.

<a id="lines-that-reappear-after-cleaning"></a>
### Lines that reappear after cleaning

A dirty squeegee blade or overloaded microfiber can put residue back on the pane. Wipe the blade dry after every stroke, rotate cloths as soon as they feel greasy, and replace rubber that has nicks or no longer maintains even contact.

Cloudy film on a coated pane requires caution. Use ammonia-free cleaner and a soft pad, and avoid abrasive scrubbing that can permanently damage the surface.

<a id="maintenance-schedules-and-common-questions"></a>
## Maintenance Schedules and Common Questions

A fixed calendar misses the conditions that put streaks back on glass. Inland homes collect dust and seasonal debris, while coastal properties accumulate salt haze and moisture in screens, tracks, and frames. After pollen, construction dust, or wildfire ash, an extra rinse often does more than waiting for the next scheduled visit.

| Environment | Frequency | Best Method |
|---|---|---|
| Inland homes | Four cleans per year | Interior squeegee work, exterior scrub and rinse |
| Coastal homes | Six cleans per year | Purified-water exterior cleaning with frame and screen detailing |
| After heavy pollen, dust, or ash | As needed after the event | Remove debris from screens, tracks, sills, and glass |

A practical annual rhythm places interior detailing in spring and fall, exterior purified-water work during warmer months, and full-system cleaning before winter. Sprinkler overspray, tree cover, and building orientation can shorten the useful life of any cleaning, even when the glass technique is sound. Clean screens, tracks, and sills on the same visit so they do not keep feeding dirt back onto the panes.

<a id="common-questions"></a>
### Common questions

**Why is glass cloudy between the panes?**  
That usually indicates a failed insulated-glass seal rather than surface dirt. Cleaning the interior and exterior faces will not remove moisture or haze trapped inside the unit.

**Can tinted or Low-E glass be cleaned normally?**  
Use an ammonia-free product and a soft tool. Avoid abrasive pads and aggressive scrubbing, especially when the manufacturer's coating or film is unknown.

**When should I stop using a ladder?**  
Use a ground-based pole system where practical for high or awkward panes. Ladder falls remain a serious window-cleaning hazard. A UK safety manual reports about **2 to 7 window cleaners killed each year in Great Britain** and roughly **20 to 30 suffering major injuries** from ladder falls, as documented in this [window-cleaning ladder safety manual](https://www.tbdavies.co.uk/pdfs/hse/window-cleaning-ladder-safety.pdf). CDC-cited U.S. data also record **836 ladder-fall fatalities from 2011 to 2016** and about **164,000 emergency or medical visits annually**.
